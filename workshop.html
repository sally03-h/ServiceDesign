1 · frame the decision

The changeover: what should the room say, ask and do when coffee becomes cocktails?

The open question, stated as a contested choice: should the changeover be a clear, stated cutover, or a soft overlap that lets daytime guests stay? Everything the team has found points at this moment, and nothing the team has found can settle it. team to confirm

Primary outcome — artifact plus decision

Artifact: a prioritized set of three redesigned changeover moments (chosen from the seven on the timeline wall), each with one chosen redesign direction.

Decision: for each of the three, an agreed owner and a first check to run within two weeks, plus a ruling on stated cutover versus soft overlap. A reasoned “no” from the Owner counts as an outcome and is carried forward as an open question with its reason.

Test for every activity: if it does not help produce the three moments, the owners, or the ruling, it is cut.

Why the team cannot resolve this alone

  • It is the owner's decision. No changeover time exists on any channel (C1). Setting one, or a table-keeping rule, is the owner's call, and the map records no access to the owner.
  • The key customer step has no evidence. Whether Alex can keep a table into the evening is inferred, not observed (P5 thin-evidence memo).
  • The trade-off is real. The dark, quiet room is the shop's asset (SR-J06, SR-J07) and also the reason one party read it as closed (SR-J08, C7). Clarity and atmosphere pull apart.
  • Staff feasibility rests on one voice (S1, answered by a team member).

Why this decision and not another

CandidateWhy it was or was not chosen
Changeover (C1, C7, M1–M6)Chosen. Touches all three personas, the owner, four channels and the table lights. Contested trade-off, owner-dependent, thin on the customer side.
Drink-ready notification (C5, M7)Not the focus. Staff already name the fix (S1-SQ23). Kept on the wall as the control moment: if the room ranks it first, the session confirms what we had, and we say so.
Coordination and handoff (C6)Folded in as M4 because the changeover is where its absence shows. As its own workshop it would rest on one staff voice.
Conflicting hours (C2) and the menu (C3)Folded into M1. Mostly a publishing fix once a changeover time exists.
2 · boundary objects

What participants work from

The D1 ecosystem map, the personas and the P5 current-state journey are rebuilt into three things a participant can touch in under a minute: a timeline wall, an evidence gallery, and role briefs. None of them shows a solution. Full printable versions: workshop-materials.html.

#MomentCurrent stateEvidenceGaps

Adaptation from the sources: the ecosystem map's 26 actors and 52 exchanges are cut to six gallery stations; the three persona journeys are cut to seven shared moments, because a participant cannot hold three parallel journeys in a 90-minute session; the map's gap IDs travel with each moment so the team can trace back. Constraint and scenario cards (AI-drafted, verified) carry their evidence IDs, or say they have none.

7 · facilitator script — the most critical activity

A6 · Converge and commit (0:56 – 1:20)

Chosen because it is where the session either produces owned decisions or dissolves into a pleasant discussion, and where the Owner's power matters most. Serif lines are said aloud; the rest is for the facilitator. Leader: A. Capture: B.

0:56
1 min

“We have [N] ideas on the wall. By twenty past, we'll have three redesigned moments, and each will have one direction, one owner and one first check. A reasoned ‘no’ counts as a result. Nothing leaves this room without an owner, or without a reason it doesn't have one.”

Do Cluster the idea cards under moment numbers while speaking. Count them aloud. Point at the clock.

0:57
5 min

“First: what has to be true for each idea to work? Put each card on the grid. Across is how sure we are that it's true, going by the evidence gallery. Up is how badly the idea fails if it's wrong. Silence for four minutes.”

Do Participants place cards on the assumption grid. B photographs it (C-IDs).

“Now look at the top left, critical and unsure. That's what our evidence can't carry. I'll read them out. Nobody defends anything yet.”

If everything lands in “sure”: “Point me to the card in the gallery that makes you sure.” Cards with no pointer move left.

If an idea needs the licence-holder, the owner's money, or a rule customers must follow: ask “who does that on a Saturday at six?” and move it up one row.

1:02
6 min

“Everyone except the Owner has three dots. Place them now, together, on the moments you think matter most. Owner, hold yours.”

Do Tally aloud once everyone but the Owner has placed. Then:

“Owner, three dots please. And a sentence for any dot that goes somewhere nobody else put one.”

Do Take the three moments with the most dots. Tie-break: the moment with more “critical and unsure” cards.

“Before we lock these in: staff roles, whose Saturday gets harder under these three?”

If all three are customer-facing and staff stay quiet: ask Sam by role, not by name, “What would you stop doing to fit this in?” (S1-SQ05: no time for lunch.)

If M7 comes out top: say so. “This confirms what staff already told us. Does anyone want to swap it for something we did not already know?” The answer is data either way.

1:08
8 min

“One commitment card per moment. Direction in one sentence. An owner who is a person in this room, not ‘we’. A first check we could do in two weeks. And what would show we're wrong.”

Do Participants fill the card in pairs, cross-role. B records the card text verbatim (C-IDs).

“Owner: for each moment, yes, yes-if, or no. If it's no, tell us what would change it.”

If the Owner says yes to everything at once: “If you could only do one, which would you drop?”

If the Owner is talking over others: “Let's hear from the roles who'd carry it first. Crossover staff, can you carry this on a shift without the licence?”

If everyone agrees too fast: “Say the strongest argument against this and I'll write it on the card.”

If the room drifts to one grievance (shouting, hours, rent): “That's on the parking-lot card and in the log. Back to who owns moment two.”

1:16
4 min

“Last step is private. On each slip, write a number from one to five for each moment, where one is ‘I'd block it’ and five is ‘I'd champion it’, and one sentence: what would make you say no? Fold it, drop it in the bowl.”

Do B reads the tallies and the sentences aloud, unattributed. Any moment with a 1 or 2 becomes an open question with that sentence attached. It is not overruled and not dropped.

“Thank you. That's our result. Capture lead will read it back.”

Do Hand to A7. If more than 2 minutes remain on the clock, spend them here, not on re-opening the discussion.

8 · risk and contingency

What we will cut, compress or swap, and how we decide

Three questions, asked silently at any decision point

  • Can the group still produce the artifact and the decision without this?
  • What evidence or voice do we lose if we cut it?
  • Who is not being heard right now?

Never cut: the consent step in A1, and any of A6 except the grid, which compresses to three minutes. Cut order when running over: held buffer → A3 limited to M3–M5 → A5 to one scenario → A4 to H1 and H3 only. The capture lead calls the clock at 0:42, 0:56 and 1:10, and the lead decides aloud so the room hears the trade.

9 · team roles and capture

Who does what, and how we swap

Facilitator A

Leads A1–A4, A6, A7. Captures and keeps time during A5.

Facilitator B

Captures, observes and manages time during A1–A4, A6, A7. Leads A5, the service staging, while A takes capture.

Swapping on A5 is deliberate: it is the activity where the leader needs to be comfortable improvising scenes, and it gives each of us one real turn in each seat. Capture uses the four sheets in Materials §6. Every output gets a letter-and-number ID the moment it exists (G gallery, W walk-through, D diverge, S staging, C converge, X dissent, I improv). Walls are photographed before anything is cleared. Session evidence is submitted unedited.

11 · after the session

Scaffolding for synthesis and reflection

Synthesis one-pager — shape

  • Key findings — each cites at least two raw output IDs and says whether it confirms or changes a prior. Findings are not restated outputs.
  • Before / after — the table in §10, with the “After” column filled for the three chosen moments and “unchanged” stated plainly for the rest.
  • Decisions with owners — from the commitment cards, including conditional yes/no.
  • Open questions — dissent sentences, conditional-needs-owner items, and every “?” sticker cluster that no one could answer.
  • Weight — say how much of each finding rests on IMPROV statements, and that no real owner or evening customer has yet been heard.

Individual reflection — 400–600 words each

  • What I designed versus what happened.
  • One moment where I adapted in real time, and why.
  • What I would change.
  • Written separately, before comparing notes. Compare the design pack with the run log from the adaptation sheet.

Do not write these from this pack; draw them from the capture sheets.

Honest limits to carry into the write-up

  • Speculative participants are not the owner, staff or customers. Their agreement is evidence about the design, not about the shop.
  • If the session only confirms the priors, say so and explain why that is still informative: it narrows the open question to what only the owner can answer.
  • Energy in the room is not an outcome. The outcome is a changed journey or a closed decision.
